## The question

What is the strongest opening for market entry. The founder's proposal was an all-peptides,
all-people, all-protocols observational study at scale, aimed at Gen Z and millennials, with
creators invited across from TikTok and Instagram onto a platform that would validate them.

## Where it landed

Peptides are the right category, for a stronger reason than market size. On 24 July 2026 the FDA's
Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee recommended six peptides for the 503A bulks list over its
own staff's objection on all six, after those staff had written that there is a lack of evidence to
evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the compounds under review. Notice-and-comment rulemaking
must now run, with a public docket. No registry of users exists for any of the six.

Four acts, two of them concurrent. A registry on the six compounds, published and filed into the
docket. A register running underneath it on the lawful peptide lane, where randomised arms can be
sold. Creators as study subjects once the first study publishes. A payer and employer panel as the
conflict-free hedge.

## What changed during the work, and is left in the record

- **All-protocols** was read as assigning regimens, which would have been a drug-law problem. It
  means recording regimens people already run. The objection was withdrawn.
- **"Peptides cannot pay you"** was too broad. It holds for the injectables and not for the lawful
  lane, which is a real market with real brands.
- **The IRB route** was treated as an open search when Scott Overcash already carries central-IRB
  experience across Advarra, WCG and Sabai.
- **The methods objection** on healthy-user bias is substantially answered by within-person design,
  which is what Caroline Porche's published work already does.
