## 1. The Question

Ēnel has settled that it raises on a register-and-study business with a consumer surface that feeds it. It has not settled **what it opens with**. The founder's position is an all-peptides, all-people, all-protocols institutional-review-board study, launched as a public event, aimed at Gen Z and millennials, with influencers invited to migrate from TikTok and Instagram onto a platform that would validate them.

The review of 17 August accepted the category and rejected the shape. It proposed a four-act opening. The question for this seat is whether that opening survives the company's own go-to-market doctrine, and if it does, what the sequenced plan is.

## 2. The Context

The company has been live since 21 June 2026. It adds roughly fifteen members a week on nineteen dollars a day of paid spend that is being phased to zero. Eight advisory agreements are signed. It has never run a study. There is no institutional-review-board relationship, and without one there is no research exemption under Washington's My Health My Data Act or Nevada SB 370.

**What changed in the three weeks since the last convening.** On 15 April 2026 the FDA removed twelve peptides from Category 2, the do-not-compound list. On 23 and 24 July its Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee recommended six of seven peptides for the 503A bulks list, breaking with FDA staff on every one of the six. The staff briefing documents for that meeting stated that there is a lack of evidence to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the peptides under review. Notice-and-comment rulemaking must now run: proposed rule, public comment docket, final rule, typically eight to twenty-four months, and the agency is not bound by its committee. Compounding these substances remains unlawful in the interim.

No registry of users exists for any of the six. As of March 2026 no trial of BPC-157 was recruiting. Ēnel's own peptide library holds 129 compounds and grades 54 of them at the weakest evidence level. Demand is not in question: roughly 10.1 million US peptide searches a month in January 2026, a forum of more than 200,000 members, and 83% of peptide-aware US adults in a May 2026 survey of 2,491 people saying licensed pharmacies should be allowed to compound them.

Within a year a listed company will sell these compounds at scale. Hims & Hers has guided to $3.1B to $3.3B for 2026, owns a pharmacy bought in 2025, and is preparing a peptide launch analysts have sized near $440M.

## 3. The Proposed Direction

Four acts, two of them running at once.

**Act one, the registry.** The six compounds the committee voted on, observed in people already taking them, strictly observational, about one thousand participants, founding cohort of 330 as already costed in the first $450,000. Primary outputs are the discontinuation ledger, adverse events, source of supply, and objective markers on a consented subset. Published in full including nulls, and filed as a comment when the proposed rule opens its docket. Institutional review board in place before enrolment; a Certificate of Confidentiality requested from NIH.

**Act two, the register, running underneath from month one.** Three ordinary supplement categories with high prevalence and existing brand marketing budget. Master protocol open, randomised arms available for purchase. This is where validation revenue comes from, because peptides cannot pay: their sellers are compounding pharmacies, licensed telehealth practices and grey-market resellers, none of whom may lawfully advertise efficacy.

**Act three, the creators, after act one publishes.** Subjects rather than partners. They sign before the result is known. Nothing is paid against outcome. They may display a process mark stating they were studied here and the finding is published here, never an efficacy mark.

**Act four, the panel.** Population-level consumption signal to payers, self-insured employers and pharmacy benefit managers. Tested now with four conversations, as already assigned on the prior record.

Two findings sit under all four. First, in peptides the buyer of validation is the **channel**, not the brand: licensed pharmacies and telehealth pay to be distinguished from grey-market resellers, and that customer wants the study rigorous rather than favourable. Second, the FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance holds that health-benefit claims generally require randomised controlled human trials, and that observational evidence substitutes only where experts accept it and trials are not feasible. So what a brand pays for cannot be the observation. It is the randomised arm nested inside the open cohort, and it has to actually randomise.

## 4. The Counter-Direction

Open with the register alone, on ordinary supplement categories, and skip the peptide event entirely. The argument: validation is the largest line in the plan, supplement brands have budget to protect and a documented twenty to sixty per cent cost-per-lead penalty that substantiation addresses, and none of it depends on a federal rulemaking the agency may reverse. The peptide registry produces standing and press and no revenue, in a category whose sellers cannot legally buy anything, and it spends the company's scarcest asset, which is founder attention, on the one act with no customer at the end of it.

## 5. Fixed Constraints

Standing rules, unchanged:

- No participant is ever paid to enrol in a study or to complete one. Compensation attaches only to content a participant chooses to share.
- No advertising is carried from any category Ēnel adjudicates.
- All results are published, including nulls and negatives.
- Iterations are immutable. No finished version is edited; every variant is a new file.

**The channel doctrine, from the go-to-market memo of 28 July 2026 (James Christopher).** No paid social. No influencer partnering or sponsorship. The stated grounds are that Ēnel elected to displace influencers and that paid social is where influencers operate. The financial model follows this memo and carries no influencer partnering at any point; influencers appear only as study subjects. Growth is organic: ambassadors, events, credentialed speakers, and participants reposting on their own accounts. Paid spend is phasing to zero, and acquisition cost is modelled gliding from about $7 a person to $0.13. A separate advisory input, the SignalPattern report, recommended the opposite mix (prioritise SEO and Meta, use influencer partnerships and sponsored content) and is unsourced across three hundred pages. The model follows the memo, not the report, and the contradiction has never been formally resolved by the principal.

## 6. The Argument To Be Settled

The channel doctrine and the proposed opening collide in three places, and agree in one that matters more than the collisions.

**Where they agree, and it is the strongest point on this record.** The doctrine's problem has always been that ruling out paid social and influencer sponsorship leaves organic reach carrying almost all of growth, with ambassadors and events as the named mechanism, and ambassadors are slow, local and unproven at this stage. Act one supplies the missing organic channel. A federal agency has stated in writing that the evidence does not exist, its advisers overruled it, and a public process is now open with nothing in it. That is an earned-press position rather than a bought one, on a beat the trade and general press are already covering. It is the highest-leverage organic channel available to this company and it costs no media budget. If the doctrine is right that Ēnel must grow without buying attention, then the doctrine needs act one more than act one needs the doctrine.

**Collision one, the process mark.** The doctrine forbids influencer partnering. Act three gives a creator something issued by Ēnel to display on their own account in exchange for participating. No money moves and nothing is paid against outcome, so it satisfies the letter. It may not satisfy the purpose. To an outsider, an Ēnel mark on a creator's post is Ēnel appearing in paid-social-shaped inventory by another route, and the person carrying the mark has every commercial incentive to imply more than the finding supports. The stricter reading, which the doctrine appears to require: a creator may report their own result in their own words, and Ēnel issues them nothing at all. The weaker reading: a process mark is a factual statement about participation, and forbidding it forbids a participant from saying truthfully where they were studied. **This has to be decided before the first creator is approached, because the offer cannot be renegotiated once made.**

**Collision two, recruitment channel.** Act one recruits from the places these people already are, which are peptide forums, Discords and subreddits. Those communities police promotional posting and ban it, and a study recruiting inside them can read as astroturfing whoever posts it. The doctrine rules out paid channels, so organic community recruitment is the only route left, and it is the route most likely to produce a moderator ban and a bad first impression with the exact population the study needs. There is a clean version, which is transparent researcher accounts, moderator permission sought in advance, and no incentive offered. Nobody owns it and nobody has tested whether permission is obtainable.

**Collision three, what the consumer line is now for.** The prior record assigned a kill signal for the consumer revenue line by 4 September. The review's position is that peptides do not monetise on the consumer surface either; they recruit. If that is accepted, the metric behind the kill signal changes from consumer revenue per member to enrolment yield per member, and the threshold written this month against the wrong metric would kill or spare the wrong thing.

## 7. Open Questions

1. Process mark or nothing at all: which reading of the channel doctrine governs act three, and who decides?
2. Is organic recruitment inside peptide communities obtainable with moderator permission, and has anybody asked?
3. Does act two, the register on ordinary supplements, need a named owner now, or does it wait behind act one and therefore behind founder attention?
4. If FDA reverts to its staff position and lists none of the six, does act one still stand on its own, and what is the sentence that says so?
5. What is the kill signal for the consumer line if the consumer line's purpose is enrolment rather than revenue?
6. Does the SignalPattern contradiction get formally closed, and by whom, before any of this is executed?
